Correction, 24 November 2026. This version withdraws the earlier claim that the listed G-Mesh attestations establish Byzantine fault tolerance. See the original edition.

The chapter models the AI signers as one shared fault domain. This is an assumption about correlated failures. One failure in that domain could affect all of its signer identities.

Counting voting units

In Practical Byzantine Fault Tolerance, n counts voting replicas and f bounds how many may fail together. If a design assigns one vote per fault domain, both quantities must count domains. With one potentially faulty domain, the PBFT threshold is four voting domains.

Counting by signer leads to the same conclusion. Of the 19 entries reported in the earlier chapter, one was attributed to the author and 18 to AI verifiers. If those 18 share one failure domain, up to 18 signer identities can fail together. With n = 19 signer identities and f = 18 potentially faulty identities, the PBFT inequality would require 55 signer identities.

The author attestation

The archived entry records the author’s endorsement and says Claude signed on the author’s behalf. To establish a separate voting domain, a protocol would need evidence of independent control of the voting key and decision process. The entry is an author attestation. Independent human peer review is a separate step.
